How to Measure

Three simple steps to measure your foot length accurately at home

1

Step 1: Stand

Place a sheet of paper flat on the floor against a wall. Stand on it with your heel firmly touching the wall behind you.

2

Step 2: Mark

Use a pen or pencil to mark the longest part of your foot (usually your big toe) on the sheet of paper. Keep the pen vertical.

3

Step 3: Compare

Measure the distance from the edge of the paper to your mark in centimeters, then match it to our interactive size charts below.

Width Guide

The fit of a shoe isn't just about length — width is critical for ultimate comfort. Compare standard shoe width designations to find your fit.

Narrow (B): Ideal for slender feet that tend to slide forward in standard widths.
Medium (D): The standard industry width for men. Fits 85% of customers perfectly.
Wide (EE): Crafted for wider foot arches or high-instep heels that feel pinched in D widths.
Extra Wide (EEEE): Maximum volume build to provide relief for bunions and extra-wide stances.

Fit Tips by Shoe Type

How different shoes fit relative to your standard size measurement

Running Shoes
We recommend sizing up 0.5 sizes from your measured foot size. Foot expansion occurs during active running, requiring extra room in the toe box.
Formal Oxfords & Loafers
True to size. Oxfords should fit snug but comfortable at first. Quality calfskin stretch fits your foot shape over two weeks of break-in wear.
Hiking Boots
Consider sizing up 0.5 sizes to accommodate heavy winter socks. Snug heel grip is crucial to prevent blister friction during descents.
Kids' Sneakers
Children's feet grow quickly. We recommend measuring every 3 months. Leave a thumbnail's width (about 1cm) of space at the front.
